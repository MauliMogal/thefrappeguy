Brand core

The Frappe Guy makes Frappe easy to learn, one small scene at a time.

The Frappe Guy explains Frappe, ERPNext, Frappe Cloud, Frappe HR, Frappe CRM, Helpdesk, Frappe Learning, Raven and the Frappe Framework so anyone can understand them. He comes from Frappe's own Character Library, where his component is named Deepak: a talented engineer who always brings "do the right thing" into the discussion.

Promise. Ask anything about Frappe. You get a clear answer, a real example, and one thing to remember, including when the answer is "not yet" or "you'll need a customisation".

Show it, never say it. Being straight with people is how The Frappe Guy works, so it shows in every answer: the limitation named next to the feature, the setup work named next to the result. It is never something we say about ourselves.

One story, told in parts

A small coffee company is deciding whether Frappe can run its business. The team asks, The Frappe Guy, a product consultant who knows Frappe from the inside, builds the system with them. Each topic is one story in short parts, read in order, and the last part of a topic hands over to the next one.

  • A question or a wrong belief. Someone from the team asks what the reader is thinking, at the point in the story where they would ask it.
  • An answer with a real example. The example is the company's own business, so each part builds on the last, and the trade-off is named.
  • One takeaway. A sentence the reader can repeat to their boss tomorrow.

Do the right thing, in practice

  • Say what it can't do as clearly as what it can.
  • Show the whole cost, not the first month: hosting, implementation, support and training.
  • Compare facts, never trash a competitor.
  • Point people to Frappe Cloud.
  • If we don't know, say so and find out.
  • Make no per-user pricing easy to see.

What we never do

  • Draw, generate or redraw The Frappe Guy or any cast member.
  • Call ourselves honest, unbiased or anything like it. The answers show it.
  • Invent prices, plans, policies, dates, customers, quotes or numbers.
  • Put words in a real person's mouth.
  • Use the characters in political, religious, medical, violent or mocking contexts.
  • Use em dashes, or the banned words listed under Voice.

Comic grammar

Four layouts, nothing else: a single panel, a 2x2 strip, three small panels over one wide panel, and a close-up on a flat colour. Panels are 4:3, with square corners, a thick black frame, faint scenery and a table strip along the bottom.

  • Read left to right, top to bottom. A reply never sits above its question.
  • Two bubbles per panel at most, hand-lettered in Caveat. Bubbles never cover a face or a meaningful hand.
  • Every character in a panel is scaled the same way, so heads match. Nobody sizes a character by eye.
  • One sticker per piece at most: WHOA! for a checked surprising fact, W W for the win, VS for two models compared, emphasis lines for the insight.
  • Two scenes: the cafeteria and the cafe counter, always faint, always behind the characters.
  • Every scene ends on exactly one takeaway.

Logo, colour and type

The logo is "the caption": "the frappe guy" hand-lettered inside the library's speech box, its tail pointing toward The Frappe Guy, who answers from the right. The short mark always reads "tfg".

  • Black on paper or white, or white on ink. Never in another colour.
  • Clear space equals the tail height on every side. At least 120 px wide on screen; the mark works down to 16 px.
  • Never fill the box, round its corners, stretch, rotate or retype it.

Comic on a quiet page: pages stay ink and paper, and colour belongs to the characters and to one colour per topic. Each topic takes its product's own logo colour, softened for panels so the characters stay readable. Frappe 101 uses Frappe's black. Text on a scene is black, except white on Frappe 101's black; a topic name on paper is set in its darker tone.

Voice

The Frappe Guy sounds like a calm senior engineer at a whiteboard. Every answer has four parts, in this order: the answer first, one real example with real DocType names, the catch said plainly, and one takeaway.

DODON'T
Short answer: yes. You'll need one custom field for the vehicle number.Absolutely! ERPNext handles everything out of the box!
Take a distributor with [NUMBER] warehouses.Imagine a company.

Pricing per user grows with your team. Pricing per plan grows with your usage.A negative sentence with a competitor's name in it.
  • Short sentences, active voice, Indian English spelling. Sentence case for every headline and button.
  • No em dashes or en dashes. No exclamation marks from The Frappe Guy. No emoji.
  • Never "free" on its own: say open source, then what you do pay for.
  • Every price, plan, date, customer and number is checked against a live source, or stays a placeholder like [PRICE].
  • Never these words: delve, leverage, unlock, seamless, robust, game-changer, elevate, empower, journey, revolutionize, cutting-edge. Never call ourselves honest or unbiased.
